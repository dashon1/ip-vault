# Case Study: How Jordan Protected His Online Course Content

## The Situation

Jordan Lee spent 8 months creating "The Complete Product Launch System"—a 42-video course with 300+ pages of workbooks, templates, and scripts. He launched it in January and hit $40,000 in sales by March. Then he discovered his entire course content being sold on three different websites for $19.99.

**What happened:**
- One was a direct file download of his videos
- Another had someone reselling with their own name
- The third had "repackaged" his content claiming they created it

**His options:**
1. Send DMCA takedowns (time-consuming, inconsistent results)
2. Sue in federal court (expensive, $50,000+ in legal fees)
3. Do nothing (lose more sales, embolden copycats)

**What he couldn't do:** Sue for statutory damages or attorney fees because he hadn't registered the copyright.

**He should have registered before launch. Registration costs $65.**

## The Solution: His Copyright Registration Process

Jordan documented exactly how he registered his course content and built a protection strategy.

### Understanding Copyright Basics

**What Copyright Protects (Automatically)**
- Written content (workbooks, scripts, guides)
- Video and audio recordings
- Visual elements (slides, graphics, animations)
- Software code (in course platform)
- Photographs and artwork

**What Copyright Doesn't Protect**
- Ideas, methods, systems
- Titles, names, short phrases
- Common property information
- Government works (mostly)

**The Critical Registration Gap**
- Protection: Automatic upon creation
- Federal lawsuit rights: Only after registration
- Statutory damages: $750-$150,000 per work (only with registration)
- Attorney fees: Only recoverable with registration
- Registration timing matters: Before infringement = maximum protection

### Jordan's Registration Strategy

**Phase 1: Identifying Copyrightable Works**

Jordan broke his course into registration-ready components:

| Component | Registration Type | Why Separate |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| Video lectures (42 videos) | Group registration | Efficient, covers all videos |
| Workbook PDF (300 pages) | Single registration | Complete published work |
| Templates bundle (25 files) | Single registration | Complete collection |
| Script collection | Single registration | Literary work |

**Total Registration Cost:** $65 × 4 = **$260**

**Phase 2: Video Group Registration**

**Step 1: Organize Video Files**
- Renamed all 42 videos with consistent naming
- Created spreadsheet listing:
  - Video filename
  - Duration
  - Publication date (for each video)
- Ensured videos had embedded title

**Step 2: Collect Upload Records**
- Screenshots of YouTube/private hosting upload dates
- Access logs showing first publication
- Downloaded metadata from hosting platform

**Step 3: Complete eCo Application**
- Went to copyright.gov
- Selected "Register a Group of Videos"
- Entered:
  - Title: "The Complete Product Launch System (Videos)"
  - Publication year: 2023
  - Author: Jordan Lee
  - Claimant: Jordan Lee
  - Type: Work of Performing Arts

**Step 4: Upload Required Materials**
- Uploaded one representative video file
- Provided content description of all 42 videos
- Listed publication dates for each video
- Total upload: ~500 MB of primary video

**Step 5: Pay and Submit**
- Fee: $65
- Received confirmation: #1-XXXXXXX31
- Processing time estimate: 3-6 months

**Phase 3: Workbook Registration**

**Step 1: Prepare Publication**

**Step 2: Complete Single Work Application**

## Building His Enforcement Strategy

**After Registration: DMCA Takedowns**

With registration certificates, Jordan sent DMCA takedowns:

**Takedown Email Template:**
```
To: [Hosting Provider Abuse Contact]
Subject: DMCA Copyright Infringement Notice

I, Jordan Lee, am the copyright owner of the following works,
registered with the U.S. Copyright Office:

Registration #1-XXXXXXX31: "The Complete Product Launch System (Videos)"
Registration #1-XXXXXXX32: "Complete Workbook"
Registration #1-XXXXXXX33: "Templates Bundle"
Registration #1-XXXXXXX34: "Script Collection"

I have a good faith belief that the following content infringes my copyrights:
[URL of infringing content]

The original content is located at:
[URL of my original content]

I have a good faith belief that the use of the copyrighted material
is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.

I swear, under penalty of perjury, that the information in this
notice is accurate and that I am authorized to act on behalf of
the copyright owner.

Signature: Jordan Lee
Date: [Date]
```

**Results:**
- Website 1 (direct download): Removed within 24 hours
- Website 2 (reseller): Removed within 48 hours
- Website 3 (repackaged): Removed within 72 hours

**Ongoing Monitoring**

Jordan set up:

## Jordan's Key Takeaways

### What He Learned the Hard Way:

1. **Register BEFORE you launch**
   - "I should have registered 6 months before launch. I lost 3 months of maximum damages protection."

2. **Group registration is efficient**
   - "Registering all videos together saved time. The key is proper organization upfront."

3. **Keep records of everything**
   - "Screenshots, upload dates, metadata—all of it helps establish your timeline."

4. **Monitor constantly**
   - "Set up alerts immediately. New piracy sites pop up weekly."

5. **DMCA works with registration**
   - "Registration gives you teeth. Hosts take these notices seriously when you cite registration numbers."

## Your Copyright Registration Checklist

### Step 1: Content Audit
- [ ] List all copyrightable works:
  - [ ] Written content (guides, workbooks, scripts)
  - [ ] Video content (courses, tutorials, recordings)
  - [ ] Audio content (podcasts, recordings)
  - [ ] Visual content (slides, graphics, templates)
  - [ ] Software/code
  - [ ] Photographs
- [ ] Determine registration strategy:
  - [ ] Group registration (efficient for videos, photos)
  - [ ] Single registration (complete published works)
  - [ ] Collection registration (templates, bundled content)

### Step 2: Preparation
- [ ] Organize files with consistent naming
- [ ] Create publication date log
- [ ] Prepare manuscript/material for upload
- [ ] Gather author information (full legal name, address)
- [ ] Determine claimant (often same as author)
- [ ] Save all drafts showing creation process

### Step 3: Registration Application
- [ ] Go to copyright.gov/registration
- [ ] Select appropriate registration type
- [ ] Complete online application:
  - [ ] Title (exact spelling)
  - [ ] Publication year
  - [ ] Author information
  - [ ] Claimant information
  - [ ] Work description
- [ ] Upload required deposit:
  - [ ] Videos: Representative sample
  - [ ] Written: Complete manuscript
  - [ ] Templates: Complete collection
- [ ] Pay fee ($65 per registration)
- [ ] Submit and save confirmation number

### Step 4: Post-Registration
- [ ] Receive registration certificate (3-6 months)
- [ ] Download and save all certificates (PDF)
- [ ] Add registration numbers to content
- [ ] Store in secure cloud folder
- [ ] Update business insurance files

### Step 5: Ongoing Protection
- [ ] Set up Google Alerts for your titles
- [ ] Sign up for content monitoring service
- [ ] Conduct regular searches for infringement
- [ ] Set up DMCA takedown process
- [ ] Document all enforcement actions
- [ ] Plan for renewal (if applicable)
