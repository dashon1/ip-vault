# Corporation Formation Guide

> A comprehensive walkthrough for forming a C-Corporation, ideal for startups seeking venture capital and growth-oriented businesses.

---

## Why Form a Corporation?

### Benefits of Corporation Structure

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Unlimited Growth** | Can have unlimited shareholders |
| **VC-Ready** | Standard structure for venture capital |
| **Stock Options** | Can issue various classes of stock |
| **Tax Advantages** | Lower corporate tax rate (21% flat) |
| **Perpetual Existence** | Corporation survives founder changes |
| **Credibility** | Strongest professional image |

---

## Step 1: Choose Your State

### Where to Incorporate

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Home State** | Simplicity, local compliance | Higher fees in some states |
| **Delaware** | Business-friendly laws, VC preference | Must register to do business elsewhere |
| **California** | Tech ecosystem, talent pool | High fees, complex rules |
| **Wyoming** | Privacy, low fees | Less recognized outside WY |

**VC Recommendation:** Delaware is the standard for startups seeking venture capital. Most VCs expect Delaware C-Corp.

---

## Step 2: Name Your Corporation

### Naming Requirements

**Your corporation name MUST:**
- [ ] Include "Corporation," "Corp," or "Inc."
- [ ] Be distinguishable from existing entities
- [ ] Not be too similar to existing trademarks
- [ ] Not contain restricted words

### Name Search Checklist
- [ ] Delaware Division of Corporations database
- [ ] USPTO trademark search
- [ ] Domain name availability
- [ ] Social media handle availability

---

## Step 3: Appoint Directors

### Initial Board of Directors

**Requirements:**
- [ ] Minimum 1 director (Delaware)
- [ ] Directors manage corporate governance
- [ ] Initial directors serve until first shareholder meeting
- [ ] Directors set company policy and strategy

**Considerations:**
- [ ] VCs often require board seats
- [ ] Typical cap table: 2 founders, 1 VC, 1 independent
- [ ] Directors can be changed at any time

---

## Step 4: File Certificate of Incorporation

### Required Information

**Certificate of Incorporation includes:**
- [ ] Corporation name
- [ ] Registered agent name and address
- [ ] Number of authorized shares
- [ ] Par value of stock (often $0.0001)
- [ ] Incorporator name and signature

### Filing Checklist
1. [ ] Choose number of authorized shares (typically 10M)
2. [ ] Draft Certificate of Incorporation
3. [ ] Pay Delaware filing fee: $89 (online)
4. [ ] Submit to Delaware Division of Corporations
5. [ ] Receive certified copy

---

## Step 5: Create Corporate Bylaws

### What Bylaws Include

| Section | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Board Composition** | Number of directors, terms |
| **Officers** | Titles, duties, appointment |
| **Meetings** | Frequency, notice requirements |
| **Voting** | Quorum, voting rights |
| **Shares** | Rights, transfers, dividends |
| **Amendments** | How to change bylaws |

### Bylaws Checklist
- [ ] Define board structure
- [ ] Specify officer roles (CEO, CFO, etc.)
- [ ] Outline meeting procedures
- [ ] Include stock transfer restrictions
- [ ] Add indemnification provisions

---

## Step 6: Issue Initial Stock

### Founder Stock Allocation

**Common Approach:**
- [ ] Common stock to founders (typically 4-10M shares each)
- [ ] Vesting schedule (4 years with 1 year cliff)
- [ ] 83(b) election filing within 30 days

### Stock Issuance Checklist
- [ ] Determine share allocation
- [ ] Draft stock purchase agreements
- [ ] Issue restricted stock grants
- [ ] File 83(b) elections
- [ ] Update cap table

---

## Step 7: Hold First Board Meeting

### Required Actions

**First Board Meeting agenda:**
- [ ] Adopt corporate bylaws
- [ ] Elect officers (CEO, CFO, Secretary)
- [ ] Approve stock issuance
- [ ] Designate bank and signers
- [ ] Authorize corporate actions

**Documentation:**
- [ ] Minutes of meeting
- [ ] Consent resolutions (alternative)
- [ ] Stock ledger

---

## Step 8: Get an EIN

### Employer Identification Number

**How to Get:**
1. [ ] Go to IRS.gov
2. [ ] Apply online (free, instant)
3. [ ] Select "C-Corporation" as entity type
4. [ ] Receive EIN immediately

---

## Step 9: Open Bank Account

### Required Documents

- [ ] Certificate of Incorporation
- [ ] EIN confirmation
- [ ] Corporate bylaws
- [ ] Board resolution authorizing account
- [ ] Government ID for signatories

### Banking Checklist
- [ ] Research startup-friendly banks
- [ ] Compare services and fees

## Corporation Tax Structure

### C-Corporation Taxation

**Double Taxation:**
1. Corporation pays tax on profits (21% federal rate)
2. Shareholders pay tax on dividends (capital gains rate)

**Deductions Available:**
- [ ] Salaries and benefits
- [ ] Rent and utilities
- [ ] Equipment and supplies
- [ ] Professional services
- [ ] Research and development

### Estimated Tax Payments

| Quarter | Due Date |
|---------|----------|
| Q1 | April 15 |
| Q2 | June 15 |
| Q3 | September 15 |
| Q4 | December 15 |

## VC Funding Considerations

### Why VCs Prefer C-Corps

| Feature | Benefit |
|---------|---------|
| **Stock Classes** | Can create preferred shares for investors |
| **Option Pool** | Can reserve shares for employee equity |
| **Convertible Notes** | Standard funding instruments |
| **Liquidation Preference** | Investor protection mechanisms |

### Funding Timeline

```
Seed: $500K - $2M (usually convertible note)
Series A: $2M - $10M (priced round)
Series B+: Growth capital (scaled rounds)
```
