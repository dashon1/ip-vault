# Case Study: How a Tech Startup Raised $1.2M with Proper Structure

## The Situation

In 2022, Sarah Kim and her co-founder Tom were building "HealthTrack"—an app that helped users manage chronic health conditions. They had:
- Built an MVP with $40,000 of personal savings
- Grown to 5,000 beta users
- Received interest from 3 angel investors

The problem: They were operating as a California LLC. Every investor they met asked the same question:

**"Have you incorporated? We're going to need you to convert to a Delaware C-Corp before we can invest."**

Two investors walked away while Sarah and Tom figured out how to restructure.

**They could have had investor-ready structure from day one for $1,200.**

## The Solution: Complete Entity Restructuring

Sarah documented the entire conversion process so you don't make the same mistake.

### Understanding Why Investors Require Delaware C-Corps

**The Investor Perspective**
- VC funds typically have:
  - LP investors who need pass-through taxation
  - Portfolio company management needs
  - Standardized documents and structures
  - Clean equity cap tables

**Delaware C-Corp Advantages**
| Feature | LLC | Delaware C-Corp |
|---------|-----|----------------|
| Investor-friendly equity | Complex | Standard (common/preferred) |
| Stock options | Difficult | Easy (ISOs, RSUs) |
| Venture capital compatibility | Poor | Excellent |
| Acquisition structure | Complicated | Clean |
| IPO readiness | Not possible | Standard |
| Tax treatment | Pass-through | Corporate (but VC-friendly) |

### The Conversion Decision

**Option A: Convert Existing LLC to C-Corp**
- Pros: Preserve history, simpler
- Cons: Complex conversion in California, potential tax implications
- Cost: $2,000-$5,000 in legal/filing fees

**Option B: Form New Delaware C-Corp, Transfer Assets**
- Pros: Clean structure, standard documents
- Cons: Need new EIN, transfer agreements
- Cost: $1,200-$2,000

**Option C: Start Fresh (Best for Pre-Revenue Startups)**
- Pros: Maximum simplicity, clean cap table
- Cons: Lose LLC history (usually minimal at early stage)
- Cost: $1,200-$1,500

**Sarah's Decision:** Option B - Form new Delaware C-Corp, transfer IP and assets

### Phase 1: Delaware C-Corp Formation

**Step 1: Delaware Name Search**
- Searched Delaware Division of Corporations database
- Checked: "HealthTrack Inc," "HealthTrack Technologies Inc," "HT Technologies Inc"
- Found: "HEALTHTRACK INC" - Available
- Reserved name: Not needed for immediate filing

**Step 2: Registered Agent Selection**
- Researched Delaware registered agents
- Selected: Harvard Business Services ($199/year)
- Criteria: Reputation, online tools, compliance reminders

**Step 3: Certificate of Incorporation Preparation**

**Standard Delaware C-Corp Certificate Includes:**
- Company name
- Registered agent information
- Number of authorized shares
- Par value of stock (often $0.0001)
- Incorporator information

**HealthTrack's Certificate:**
```
CERTIFICATE OF INCORPORATION
OF
HEALTHTRACK INC.

FIRST: The name of the corporation is HealthTrack Inc.

SECOND: The registered office of the corporation in the
State of Delaware is located at 16192 Coastal Highway,
Lewes, Delaware 19958. The name of the registered agent
is Harvard Business Services, Inc.

THIRD: The purpose of the corporation is to engage in
any lawful act or activity for which corporations may
be organized under the General Corporation Law of the
State of Delaware.

FOURTH: The total number of shares of stock which the
corporation shall have authority to issue is 10,000,000
shares of Common Stock, par value $0.0001 per share.

FIFTH: The incorporator is Sarah Kim, with an address
of [Redacted], who is 18 years of age or older.

IN WITNESS WHEREOF, the incorporator has executed this
Certificate of Incorporation this [Date] day of [Month], 2023.

_________________________
Sarah Kim, Incorporator
```

**Step 4: Filing**
- Submitted online to Delaware Division of Corporations
- Fee: $89 (standard filing)
- Expedited: $100 additional for same-day processing
- Total: $189
- Received: Certified copy + original filing

**Step 5: First Organizational Steps**

**Day 1 Post-Incorporation:**
- Held first board meeting (via video call)
- Adopted bylaws
- Appointed officers (Sarah as CEO, Tom as CTO)
- Authorized initial stock issuance
- Opened bank account

**Stock Issuance Example:**
```
RESOLVED, that the corporation issue the following shares
of Common Stock to its founders:

- 4,000,000 shares to Sarah Kim (40%)
- 4,000,000 shares to Tom Rodriguez (40%)
- 2,000,000 shares to the Employee Stock Option Pool (20%)

Total issued: 10,000,000 shares
```

### Phase 2: IP and Asset Transfer

**Critical Step: Clean IP Ownership**

This was the most important step for investors. Sarah and Tom had built HealthTrack while employed elsewhere, so they needed to:

1. **Document the IP Creation**
   - Gathered all development records
   - Showed work was done on personal time
   - Documented no company resources were used

2. **Execute IP Assignment Agreement**
   ```
   INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY ASSIGNMENT

   For valuable consideration, the receipt of which is hereby
   acknowledged, the undersigned hereby irrevocably assigns
   to HealthTrack Inc. all right, title, and interest in
   and to the following:

   - HealthTrack mobile application
   - All source code and documentation
   - All trademarks and domain names
   - All related IP developed during [Date Range]

   Signed: Sarah Kim, Date
   Signed: Tom Rodriguez, Date
   ```

3. **Assign Domain Names**
   - Transferred healthtrack.app to HealthTrack Inc.
   - Updated registration to corporate account

4. **Trademark Assignment**
   - Applied for "HealthTrack" trademark in company name
   - Assigned any existing common law rights to corporation

## Key Takeaways

### Sarah's Lessons Learned:

1. **Form as C-Corp from day one if you might raise VC**
   - "We saved $40K and 6 weeks by not forming an LLC first. If you think you'll raise money, just incorporate as a Delaware C-Corp."

2. **Clean IP is non-negotiable**
   - "Every investor's first question after term sheets: 'Are the founders assigning all IP to the company?' Get this right early."

3. **Standard documents save time and money**
   - "We used Y Combinator's SAFE documents and standard NVCA documents. Investors knew exactly what they were getting."

4. **The option pool matters**
   - "We reserved 20% for employees. Investors want to see you can attract talent without diluting founders too much more."

5. **California has additional requirements**
   - "Operating in California means registering as a foreign corporation. Don't forget this—it's required by law."
